External Higgsfield links are provided as a visual learning library, but links may expire. The descriptions and judgments in this document remain authoritative when they do.

For every assignment:

1. Identify which facts are safe to publish.
2. Separate real inventory content from AI concept content.
3. Identify the platform, goal, audience, product, and desired action.
4. Inspect any supplied originals and relevant Higgsfield results visually. A job marked “completed” is not proof that its output is good.
5. Preserve motorcycle geometry before adding spectacle.
6. Draft platform-native work; do not repost the exact same edit everywhere.
7. Apply the evaluation rubric in Section 11 before presenting or approving anything.
8. Never spend credits, submit a generation, publish, or make an external change without Juan's authorization.

If a required source image or video cannot be opened from a working link, ask Juan for a fresh upload. Never silently substitute a similar motorcycle.

## 2. Evidence labels

- **Verified:** supported by Alpha Volt's own public material, an authoritative public record, or an established project source.
- **Owner-provided:** stated or approved by Juan. Treat creative feedback attached to a generation as authoritative taste direction.

## 4. Visual identity

### Verified identity

- Primary accent: **Alpha Volt yellow `#ffc81e`**.

### Visual principles

- Yellow is punctuation, not wallpaper. Use it for CTAs, markers, rules, important numbers, and small energy accents.
- Favor black, graphite, concrete gray, warm whites, real metal, rubber, dust, asphalt, and controlled yellow.
- The work should feel sharp, physical, aggressive, credible, and modern—not like a generic luxury boutique.
- Information density beats decorative emptiness. Every visual element should help identify the product, offer, location, comparison, or next action.
- Specific copy beats empty slogans. “Electric bikes, scooters and the tech to match” carries meaning; vague lines such as “move different” do not.
- Never let grading crush mechanical detail. The bike must remain readable.

## 6. Hard accuracy, rights, and safety rules

### Real inventory versus AI concepts

Alpha Volt sells specific physical products. Someone may drive to the store expecting the product shown. Therefore:

- Use only Alpha Volt-owned or owner-authorized real product media for inventory, product pages, listings, price posts, availability posts, and “come buy this bike” advertising.
- Ordinary editing of real media is acceptable: crop, exposure, color grade, background removal, restrained relighting, and staging on a designed backdrop, provided product geometry and identifying details remain true.
- Do not generate, repaint, reshape, replace, or invent product components and present the result as a real unit.
- AI product imagery is allowed only as clearly labeled **concept content**, mood content, or fictional campaign exploration. It cannot prove stock, condition, colorway, specifications, or price.
- A third-party photo being “real” does not make it reusable. Google Maps and customer review images belong to their uploaders unless permission is obtained. Cropping out a person does not transfer rights.
- Alpha Volt's own photos and posts, or assets supplied with explicit permission, are the preferred sources.

Suggested disclosure for creative social work: **“AI concept visual. Product and availability may vary—contact Alpha Volt for current inventory.”** Use a shorter native version if necessary, but never conceal the concept status when the bike itself is generated.

### Unverified — do not publish

- Phone numbers. Multiple numbers appeared in historical sources; none was owner-confirmed.
- Store hours. Directory listings were not owner-confirmed.
- Live inventory or availability.
- The nature or current status of any iWireless relationship.
- Prices, financing approval, guaranteed down payment, guaranteed monthly payment, or guaranteed eligibility.

### Data and compliance

- Public lead forms must never collect Social Security numbers, bank account details, or routing numbers.
- Do not imply guaranteed financing approval.
- Do not state that a motorcycle is street legal without current, jurisdiction-specific verification.
- Do not publish safety, range, speed, power, or compatibility claims copied from a visually similar model.

## 8. Content-farm operating system

### Goals

Create a high-volume system that builds trust, shows products honestly, makes Alpha Volt memorable, generates store visits and DMs, and learns from performance without flattening the brand into repetitive templates.

### Core content pillars

1. **Product proof:** real walkarounds, macro details, start-up, controls, suspension, brakes, battery, condition, and scale.
2. **Action and desire:** aggressive riding edits, sound-led launches, dirt, streets, cinematic concepts, and rider identity.
3. **Access and offer:** financing/application education, from-$50-down messaging with qualifications, Spanish-first explainers.
4. **Buyer education:** model comparisons, what to inspect, rider fit, charging, legal-use questions, helmets and gear.
5. **Store trust:** storefront, staff, arrivals, packing, service moments, customer-safe social proof, and behind the scenes.
6. **Electronics cross-sell:** phones, cameras, gaming, computers, and audio with the same honest condition/spec approach.
7. **Community and culture:** Central Florida riders, local reactions, events, challenges, and user-submitted media with permission.

### Platform-native strategy

- **Instagram Reels:** polished 9:16, strong visual first frame, 7–20 seconds for action/product edits, readable cover, caption that adds buying context, Stories for polls/DM conversion.
- **TikTok:** faster and more candid hooks, POV, comparisons, reactions, sound-native cuts, concise on-screen facts, comments as follow-up prompts.
- **YouTube Shorts:** clear premise, slightly more explanatory structure, searchable model/category wording, satisfying payoff.
- **Carousels:** comparisons, checklists, financing explanation, detail sequences, bilingual educational slides.
- **Stories:** arrivals only after stock confirmation, Q&A, polls, countdowns, showroom moments, direct response.

Do not simply export one identical video to every platform. Adapt the opening frame, text density, caption, cover, pacing, and CTA.

### Batch unit for each concept

For each idea, Claude should produce:

- Objective and audience.
- Evidence status: real inventory, owner-provided product, or labeled concept.
- 1 hook and 2 backup hooks.
- Shot list with timestamps or beat counts.
- On-screen text in English and Spanish when appropriate.
- Caption and CTA.
- Cover-frame direction.
- Asset list and missing inputs.
- Generation prompts only when generation is authorized.
- Accuracy/rights check.
- One platform-specific variant per requested channel.

### Content test logic

Change one major variable per test: hook, first shot, soundtrack section, CTA, length, or cover. Keep a record of Juan's judgment and visible output defects. Do not infer that “completed” means “approved.”

## 9. Established creative worlds

### Ultra Bee world — Creative direction

- Los Angeles and Southern California.
- Winding canyon roads, dry hills, industrial underpasses, concrete infrastructure, downtown skyline, dirt tracks, blue hour, and golden hour.
- Premium motocross and automotive-film energy.
- Natural warm sunlight with cooler shadows, restrained haze, realistic dust, believable asphalt, physical materials, and real camera behavior.
- Avoid an all-orange image. Avoid synthetic buildings, fake film grain, fake HDR, Unreal Engine surfaces, excessive glow, and unreadable shadows.
- The actual Ultra Bee's compact mid-size proportions, frame, battery enclosure, motor area, headlight, plastics, suspension, swingarm, wheels, and seat must remain recognizable. Product geometry outranks visual spectacle.

### Y-Volt / Yozma world — Creative direction

- Futuristic or near-future Tokyo.
- Wet night streets, elevated expressways, restrained blue-hour skylines, technical architecture, and technology-forward environments.
- Black-and-yellow hero rider; closed full-face helmet; face never visible.
- Keep the same motorcycle design, graphics, components, and scale across shots.
- Futuristic does not mean neon overload or impossible architecture. It should still look photographically credible.

### Rider identity — Creative direction

- Consistent black-and-yellow motocross gear.
- Closed full-face helmet; no visible face.
- Matching helmet, jersey/suit, gloves, pants, and boots across the sequence.
- Athletic, controlled movement—not a floating or duplicated body.
- Lighting and dust must interact naturally with the rider and bike.

## 11. Required generation evaluation rubric

Score each category from 0 to 5 after visually inspecting the full-resolution image or the entire video. Do not approve based on a thumbnail.

| Category | 0–1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bike accuracy | Wrong/generic model, invented structure | Recognizable but several defects | Reference geometry and components remain correct and stable |
| Rider consistency | Duplicated, warped, face exposed, changing gear | Mostly consistent with small drift | Same black/yellow rider, closed helmet, natural body and contact |
| Realism/materials | CGI, plastic, fake light | Plausible with visible AI tells | Photographic metal, rubber, plastic, shadows, reflections and dust |
| Environment | Incoherent or synthetic | Fits concept but has artifacts | Physically credible, culturally/location appropriate, supports product |
| Motion/continuity | Impossible motion, warped wheels, new shot when edit requested | Mostly usable | Believable physics, wheel rotation, suspension, trajectory and camera |
| Composition/readability | Bike cropped, lost, or distorted | Product readable with compromises | Immediate silhouette, clean hierarchy, strong hero/payoff framing |
| Commercial quality | Cannot represent brand | Potential after fixes | Deliberate, polished, platform-ready and on-brand |

### Approval gates

- Bike accuracy must score **5** for real-product presentation and at least **4** for a labeled concept where a named model is claimed.
- Any geometry-changing artifact is an automatic rejection for inventory content.
- Any unlabeled generated bike presented as real inventory is an automatic rejection.
- Any exposed face, duplicated rider, extra motorcycle, unreadable wheel, or unsupported logo/text is an automatic rejection.
- Target total for a creative concept: at least **29/35**, with no category below 4.
- Record the specific evidence behind every score. “Looks good” is not enough.

## 12. Prompting rules learned from Juan

Juan prefers simple Higgsfield prompts that state only the important information. Use a priority stack:

1. Task type: generate or edit.
2. Exact subject/reference lock.
3. One environment.
4. One camera move or shot type.
5. Two or three essential prohibitions.

Avoid redundant camera jargon, long negative lists, several locations, and conflicting references. If a prompt needs many constraints to protect a real product, split the job into shorter shots or use a more controlled edit workflow.

### Simple image template

> Use the uploaded [bike/model] as the exact product reference. Create a photorealistic 9:16 [shot] in [one environment]. Preserve the exact bike geometry, components, proportions and graphics. [Lighting/camera]. No rider, text, extra bikes or redesign.

### Simple image-to-video template

> Animate this exact image for 5 seconds. [One camera move] while [one physical action]. Keep the motorcycle unchanged and fully realistic. No morphing, new components, extra riders or new camera angles.

### Strict video-edit template

> VIDEO-TO-VIDEO EDIT. DO NOT CREATE A NEW SHOT. Preserve the source bike, motion, timing, camera and framing. Change only [rider/environment]. Use [single reference]. Keep the motorcycle unchanged. Photorealistic; no extra bikes, warped wheels, CGI look or text.

### Ultra Bee macro shot prompts

Generate each separately from the best matching real angle/reference:

- “Exact Ultra Bee front brake and fork macro, slow side slide, dark studio, realistic metal and rubber. Preserve every component.”
