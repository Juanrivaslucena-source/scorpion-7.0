# Finding Prospects

## The qualifier

You're looking for one specific gap, visible in about ten seconds:

> **Publishes long-form consistently. Publishes little or no short-form.**

That's it. Open their YouTube channel, click the **Shorts** tab. Empty or stale while the Videos
tab is active weekly? That's a prospect. Same check on TikTok/Instagram: a few posts from months
ago, then nothing.

This gap means three things at once: they make enough content to clip, they haven't solved
short-form, and they already know they should be doing it. You're not creating demand, you're
removing an excuse.

## Who to target

**Best fit — creators at 5k–100k subscribers.**

Below 5k they usually can't pay. Above ~100k they already have an editor and a process. The
middle band has revenue but no team, which is exactly the shape of client who hires one
freelancer.

In rough order of how well they convert:

1. **Podcasters** — long episodes, obvious clip moments, most already know clips matter. Best
   single category.
2. **Coaches / consultants / course sellers** — clips are lead generation, so they think in
   money rather than views. Highest budgets. Pitch leads, not reach.
3. **Interview / commentary YouTubers** — dense with standalone moments.
4. **Newsletter writers with a podcast** — often completely ignore video.
5. **Local businesses with a dead TikTok** — lowest budgets, but easy yes and quick to close.

## Where to find them

- **YouTube search** by niche + "podcast", then sort by upload date. Filter to recent uploads so
  you know they're active. This is the highest-yield source; most of your list comes from here.
- **Apple Podcasts / Spotify** category charts — work *down* from the top. The top 20 have teams;
  ranks 50–200 are the band you want.
- **Podchaser / Listen Notes** — searchable by category and size, with contact info more often
  exposed than on YouTube.
- **X and LinkedIn** — search "my podcast" or "new episode" and see who's posting episodes with
  no clips.
- **YouTube "related channels"** — once one prospect fits, the sidebar is full of similar ones.
  This is how you go from 5 prospects to 50 fast.

## Pick one niche and stay in it

Do not send to 60 random creators across 60 topics. Pick one — fitness podcasts, or business
coaches, or true crime — and work only that for the first week.

Three reasons, all practical:
- Your clips get better fast because you learn what lands in that niche.
- One clip's hook style can be reused across prospects, cutting your per-prospect time.
- Referrals only happen inside a niche. Creators know other creators who do the same thing.

Switching niches every day resets all three.

## Finding contact details

- YouTube: **About** tab → "View email address" (needs a captcha, works fine).
- Podcast sites almost always have a contact or booking page.
- Instagram/X DMs are fine for smaller creators; email is better above ~50k.
- If you can only find a generic `info@`, send it anyway but address a person by name.

**Don't** buy scraped email lists, and don't use tools that guess addresses. It's spam, it burns
your domain reputation, and the reply rate is worse than doing it by hand.

## Volume

At roughly 20 minutes per prospect — watch, cut a clip, personalise, send — you'll manage
**15–25 per day** at a real quality bar, not the 40–60 that sounds achievable on paper. Ten
excellent, genuinely personalised messages beat sixty templated ones, and this is the single
place people talk themselves into doing the lazy version.

Expect **1–3%** to convert. That math means your first client may not arrive until 50+ contacts
in. This is normal and not a signal to change the approach — most people quit at 20 and conclude
outreach doesn't work.

## Track it

One sheet, six columns: **name · channel link · date sent · clip link · replied · outcome**.

At this volume you *will* forget who you contacted and double-message someone. That's the one
error that unmistakably marks you as a bot and loses a prospect who was otherwise interested.
